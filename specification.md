# Danville

## Current playable game

Danville is a browser-based, client-side 2D game template using Phaser 4, TypeScript and Vite. It runs locally or as static assets on Cloudflare Workers, with no gameplay backend.

The scene is a grassy field with a winding cobblestone street network and one boy with dark brown centre-parted curtain hair, a white T-shirt with a red dragon on the back, black jeans and black skate shoes with white soles. The paths follow a hand-drawn town layout, with a small central green, connecting loops and branches towards the field edges. Paths are decorative and freely walkable, with the same movement speed as grass; there are no buildings yet. It uses a slightly elevated top-down view in the style of classic farming games, rather than a diamond isometric projection. A 480 × 320 logical canvas scales to fit the browser while preserving its aspect ratio and using nearest-neighbour pixel rendering. Extra space is letterboxed. A small HTML hint explains the controls.

Arrow keys move the player in eight directions; two adjacent keys select a diagonal facing and move at the same overall speed as a single key. Opposing keys cancel one another. Walking animates in the facing direction; releasing the keys leaves the player standing and facing the last direction, including diagonals. Releasing one of two adjacent keys returns to the remaining key's cardinal facing. Right-facing views mirror the corresponding left-facing artwork, including both diagonal pairs. Movement uses elapsed time and is bounded so the entire sprite remains within the field. Long frames are limited to 50 ms of movement to prevent jumps after interruptions. The field is 2,400 by 1,600 logical pixels, independent of the 480 by 320 viewport. The camera centres on the player and stops scrolling at the field's edges so it never reveals space outside the world. The player starts near the field's centre. A temporary white circle sits 48 logical pixels to the right of the player spawn point. Clicking it enters Grass Arena Level 1, a separate 960 by 640 field using the same grass and long-grass textures without cobblestone paths. The player initially spawns in its centre beneath an opaque side-on green canvas tent with its entrance facing right and uses the same movement and camera rules. A thin white outline of the current player pose is visible through the tent while its opaque pixels cover the player. The tent has solid walls around a small walkable interior: the left interior wall is 22 pixels left of spawn, with 8 pixels of room up or down. A return area occupies the leftmost 4 pixels of this interior, 18 to 22 pixels left of spawn. Only the right-hand doorway permits exit and re-entry; the rest of the right wall is solid. Once through the doorway the player draws in front of the canvas and the outline disappears. Wall collisions also apply from outside. There is no separate interior scene. A small label identifies the arena level. This is an exploration placeholder: enemies, objectives, increasing difficulty and progression through the planned five arena levels are not implemented. The Grassland field sleeps while the arena is active, preserving its player position. The map is disabled in Grass Arena Level 1: M has no effect and the controls hint omits it. Walking left from the arena starting position into the return area inside the tent returns to Grassland. The player starts 18 pixels to the right of its nearest edge. Walking outside to the left of the tent does not trigger a return. Grassland wakes at its preserved position and the arena stops, so the next visit starts inside the tent again. The tent walls are the only collidable obstacles; there is no saving, audio or touch movement.

Pressing P pauses gameplay and freezes the current field or arena, including walking animations. A dimmed overlay shows two vertically stacked buttons centred in the game view: Play above Options. Clicking Play or pressing P again closes the menu and resumes gameplay. Options is a disabled placeholder for future settings. Holding P does not repeatedly toggle the menu.

Pressing M during gameplay in the original Grassland field opens an arena map covering the entire browser view, including any letterboxing, and hides the controls hint. Its connected landscape blends woodland in the upper left, a rustic brown town in the centre, a grey city in the upper right, grassland in the lower left and a sandy beach with sea along the lower right. Five labelled circular or oval buttons follow the supplied layout. Each time the map opens, no arena is selected or given keyboard focus; focus starts on the map dialog. Their terrain becomes more saturated on hover or keyboard focus; the grassland button uses the field's existing grass texture. Clicking Grassland closes the map and returns to the starting grass-and-cobblestone field at its preserved player position, putting the arena to sleep if necessary. The other arena buttons mark their arena as selected, with a light outline; their levels are not implemented yet. Tab navigates the buttons and Enter or Space activates them. The current field or arena and its walking animations remain paused while the map is open. Pressing M again resumes the scene that opened the map. Holding M does not repeatedly toggle the map. The pause menu and map are separate screens; neither opens while the other is active.

## Architecture

- `src/main.ts` creates the Phaser game, its scaling settings and one shared gameplay scene instance for each configured arena level, in addition to the initial Grassland scene.
- `src/FieldScene.ts` supplies the shared gameplay scene for Grassland and configured arena levels. Each instance has its own player state, bounds and terrain chunks, while sharing loaded art and walking animations. Optional paths, a configurable clickable entrance and an optional tent control which features appear. The tent is drawn above a player standing behind it; passing through the right doorway or standing below its base draws the player above it. Cached alpha data detects actual sprite overlap, and a separate outline sprite follows the player frame and mirroring above the tent. Entrance activation switches scenes, sleeping the source and starting or waking the destination. Path texture names include the scene key to avoid collisions. It loads assets, reads keyboard input, follows the player with the camera and renders nearby terrain, player and shadow. Movement state retains fractional positions for frame-rate-independent speed; the drawn player and shadow use the same whole-pixel position, which the camera follows so terrain scrolls on a consistent pixel grid.
- `src/terrain.ts` selects buffered terrain chunks and generates seeded grass placement independently of Phaser. Only chunks covering the camera view and a one-chunk border are loaded; distant chunks are destroyed and regenerated on return. Ground tiles and tuft images share loaded textures. Generation and memory use depend on the view size rather than the total field size.
- `src/paths.ts` rounds route bends with sampled quadratic curves that meet adjacent straight sections tangentially, without overshooting the control points. Curved connections round the inside corners where open route ends meet neighbouring paths. It checks path clearance independently of Phaser. The scene draws these curves with a world-aligned cobblestone pattern and narrow earth edging into disposable chunk textures. Grass clearance uses the same curves, including junction connections, preventing tufts from overlapping paths. Path textures are released when their chunks unload.
- `src/PauseScene.ts` displays the pause menu while its caller scene is paused, and resumes that scene on request. Both scenes share the game's scaling settings.
- `src/MapScene.ts` owns the map lifecycle while its caller scene is paused. It creates a full-viewport native HTML dialog with textured arena buttons, styled by `src/style.css`, and removes it when the map closes. The dialog keeps keyboard focus within its buttons. The landscape preserves a 3:2 aspect ratio with dark green letterboxing, scaling independently of the game canvas, so trees and arena circles are not stretched on tall or wide screens. Placeholder arena selection lasts until the map closes; the configured Grassland destination wakes the existing field scene. Both map and pause receive their caller scene key when opened, so closing them resumes the correct level.
- `src/tent.ts` builds five wall rectangles from the tent footprint, wall thickness and right-door opening. Walls expand by the configured foot radius. `src/movement.ts` sweeps movement on each axis to block walls, prevent tunnelling and allow sliding without a physics dependency.
- `src/spriteOutline.ts` derives a one-pixel transparent outline sheet from player alpha, keeping each animation frame separate. It adds no new player artwork or renderer-specific effects.
- `src/movement.ts` holds the movement and boundary rules independently of Phaser. Node's built-in test runner verifies these rules with `npm test`.
- `src/content/game.json` defines viewport and field dimensions separately, terrain chunk size, ground, path and tall-grass settings, player appearance, animation frame indices, walking speed, spawn position and the arena map's background and buttons.
- `public/assets/` contains PNG images and a pixel-art SVG sheet loaded by Phaser. All assets are local; gameplay makes no external service calls.

## Editing content

`map` in `src/content/game.json` defines the landscape `image` and an `arenas` list. Each arena has a unique `id`, display `label`, and `x`, `y`, `width` and `height` percentages of the landscape; `x` and `y` locate its upper-left corner. Keep each ellipse inside the landscape and clear of its neighbours. Buttons sample their underlying section of the landscape, except `texture: "ground"` reuses `world.ground`. Replace `public/assets/maps/arena-map.png` with another 3:2 image and adjust the percentages together to change the layout. The Grassland entry has `scene: "field"`, which closes the map and resumes the already loaded starting level. Entries without a scene remain selection placeholders. Adding a map button requires configuration only; implementing a new playable level still requires code.

`levels` lists playable arena definitions by unique `id`, display `name`, `width`, `height` and `spawn`. Optional `mapEnabled` defaults to true; setting it to false disables M and removes the map control hint for that level. Terrain and player artwork are shared with Grassland; levels have no paths or entrance unless explicitly configured using the same `paths` and `levelEntrance` shapes as the world. Adding a similar arena requires a new configuration entry and an entrance targeting its id. This defines level layouts, not difficulty or progression rules.

An optional level `tent` defines its transparent `image`, display `width` and `height` in game pixels, and `offsetFromSpawn`. The image is anchored at its bottom centre; a positive Y offset places its base below the player spawn so the player starts under its canvas. Grass Arena Level 1 uses `public/assets/props/green-tent-side.png`, displayed at 128 by 96 pixels, with its image base 32 pixels below spawn. Replace the PNG or change these settings to adjust appearance and placement without editing gameplay code. Preserve transparent padding and keep the spawn beneath opaque canvas. `tent.collision` defines `bounds` relative to spawn, `wallThickness`, a right-side `doorway` with relative `top` and `bottom`, and `playerRadius`. Keep the doorway wider than twice the player radius and align these ground-level bounds with the sprite; they describe the tent footprint, not its roof silhouette. Optional `tent.returnScene` names the destination for a `tent.returnArea`, whose left/right/top/bottom bounds are relative to level spawn. The return area tests the player foot position. Level 1 uses a narrow strip along the left interior wall and destination `"field"`; keep the spawn slightly to the right of the strip. The source arena stops on return so it can be entered afresh.

`world.levelEntrance` defines the temporary white circle through `offsetFromSpawn` (logical pixels relative to the configured player spawn) and `radius`. Its `scene` identifies the destination level (`grass-arena-1`). Move or replace this entrance without changing the arena definition. Only the original Grassland field has this entrance by default.

Change `src/content/game.json` to adjust movement speed, spawn location or field size. Positions and speed use logical game pixels and pixels per second. Keep the spawn within the field's sprite-sized margins. `viewport` controls the visible canvas dimensions; `world.width` and `world.height` control how far the player can explore. Keep the world at least as large as the viewport in each direction. `world.chunkSize` controls the size of streamed terrain sections (256 pixels by default); use a positive whole number, preferably a multiple of the grass cell size. Larger chunks load more offscreen terrain, while smaller chunks require more frequent creation and removal. The ground texture is aligned to world coordinates to keep chunk edges seamless.

The player sheet at `public/assets/characters/boy-eight-directions.png` uses 24 × 32 pixel frames arranged in three columns and five rows. Rows face down, left, up, down-left and up-left; columns show idle, one step and the other step. Frame indices start at zero and read across each row. The JSON maps all eight directions to idle frames, walking sequences and a `flipX` flag. Left/right pairs share frame indices, with `flipX: true` for the right-facing direction: eight views need only five sets of artwork. Replace the PNG with a matching sheet exported from Piskel or Aseprite, or change the frame sizes, mappings and flip flags in the JSON for a different layout. Frames must have consistent foot alignment and transparent padding. Mirroring also flips asymmetric details such as accessories, so characters that need consistent handedness can instead map directions to separate frames.

The grass image at `public/assets/terrain/grass.png` repeats across the field. Replace it with another seamless tile or change its path in the JSON. A sparse layer of darker long-grass tufts sits above the ground and beneath the player and shadow. Tufts are decoration only, with no collision, interaction or animation. Placement is procedural and generated as terrain chunks enter the buffered area around the camera: each placement cell has a configurable chance of containing one tuft, randomly positioned within that cell and kept inside the field. Tufts overlapping the paths and their edging are omitted. The seed and world cell coordinates determine placement and sprite variant, so the layout is identical after reload or revisiting an unloaded area, regardless of exploration order.

`world.paths` defines the repeating cobblestone `texture`, path `width` (40 logical pixels), `cornerRadius` (64 logical pixels), a `referenceSize` and named `routes`. The non-negative corner radius controls how far bends and junction connections round back along their approaches; individual bends are limited to half the shorter adjacent segment. Zero gives sharp centreline corners. Each route contains ordered `[x, y]` points measured against the reference size (805 × 534 for the supplied sketch); these scale to the world dimensions. For example, `{ "name": "New lane", "points": [[100, 100], [150, 120], [200, 100]] }` adds a rounded lane. Share endpoints to join routes, end a lane on another route to create a junction, or repeat the first point at the end to close a loop. Rounding cuts inside bends, so interior control points guide the shape rather than lying exactly on the path centreline. Open endpoints stay fixed; endpoints within half a path width of a neighbouring centreline receive rounded connections. Keep routes to at least two distinct points. Edit these points or add routes to change the network without code. The hand-authored pixel SVG at `public/assets/terrain/cobblestone.svg` can be edited in a vector editor or replaced with a seamless PNG through the texture setting.

`world.longGrass` in `src/content/game.json` defines the tuft sheet, frame dimensions, number of variants, placement cell size, density (a probability from 0 to 1) and seed. The transparent sheet at `public/assets/terrain/long-grass.svg` has three 10 × 12 pixel frames in a single row. It can be edited as SVG or replaced with a transparent PNG sheet exported from Piskel or Aseprite; update the JSON path and frame settings to match. Change the seed for a different layout, density for more or fewer tufts, or cell size for their spacing. Keep the cell size at least as large as both frame dimensions. Artwork provenance and source prompts live in `public/assets/ASSETS.md`. Adding new kinds of gameplay still requires code; changing these existing assets and settings does not.

## Local checks

`npm run typecheck` validates TypeScript, `npm test` checks movement rules, buffered terrain selection, route geometry, path clearance, deterministic grass generation, player-outline generation and tent collision/doorway rules, and `npm run build` validates and builds the production files into `dist/`. `npm run preview` serves that build locally. Visual appearance, keyboard input and animations also need browser playtesting.

## Deployment

Cloudflare Workers Builds connects directly to `paddy-devan/danville` on GitHub. Pushes to `main` deploy production; preview builds are enabled for every other branch. Both run `npm test && npm run build` before deployment, so failed tests, TypeScript checks or builds stop publication. Cloudflare runs `npx wrangler deploy` for production and `npx wrangler preview` for branch previews. Branch previews update independently of production.

`wrangler.jsonc` names the Worker `danville`, connects production to `https://danville.co.uk` through a Worker custom domain, enables Worker Previews with an empty `previews` block and publishes only the built `dist/` directory. Cloudflare manages the custom domain's DNS and HTTPS certificate. The production `workers.dev` address remains enabled, and branch previews use their own preview addresses. No server script or Cloudflare Vite plugin is needed. Wrangler is pinned in the development dependencies and `.node-version` selects Node 24 for Cloudflare's build environment. Repository connection, build commands and branch controls live in the Cloudflare dashboard, not a GitHub Actions workflow. Cloudflare manages build authentication; no deployment credentials belong in the repository.
